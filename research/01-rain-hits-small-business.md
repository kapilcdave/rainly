# How rain hits small businesses

Most of the hard numbers come from news anecdotes and industry footfall trackers. Only one academic study had a clean estimate (Roth Tran, below).

## Group 1: customers stay home (lost demand)

- **Ice cream and frozen yogurt shops, patio restaurants, beer gardens, food trucks.** A San Francisco patio restaurant: "the wind and the rain come in, so a lot of people stay away." No hard numbers found for ice cream specifically.
- **Events, festivals, farmers markets.** A New York festival organizer estimated rain cut revenue in half. One farm lost four Saturdays in a season.
- **Outdoor leisure** (golf, mini golf, water parks, boat tours, bike rentals, campgrounds). A Maine water taxi was down at least 75% in a wet June compared with the June before.
- **Outdoor shopping streets.** One rainy February cut UK high-street footfall 7.8%. Covered malls and retail parks lost less.

## Group 2: work can't get done (lost supply)

- **Roofing, concrete and paving, exterior painting, landscaping, excavation, solar installs.** An Austin builder lost about 30% of its work days to rain in one September; heavy equipment can't run in mud. Belgian builders lost about 5% of working days in H1 2024, double the 2022 rate.
- Contractors usually still pay crews and equipment on rain days and fall behind on deadlines. Contracts are full of disputes over what counts as "normal weather" and a "lost day."

## Businesses that gain from rain

Delivery, rideshare, indoor entertainment (theaters, bowling, arcades, museums), and after a storm: water-damage restoration, plumbers, auto body shops, roofers. Ubimo found rain may help apparel, beauty and footwear sales.

## How big the losses are

- **Best study:** Roth Tran, *Sellin' in the Rain* (Fed economist, *Management Science* 2023). Using daily store-level sales for a national apparel and sporting-goods brand, a one-standard-deviation weather shock on one day moves sales about 10% when measured over the following four weeks. Most of it is lost for good: switching to indoor stores offsets only a little, and online sales offset none.
- **Survey:** 66% of UK small businesses say they've lost revenue to bad weather, and 44% have no continuity plan.
- **Insurance gap:** regular business-interruption insurance generally doesn't cover ordinary weather-driven dips in sales.

## Why monthly-inches contracts fit badly

Businesses don't lose money in proportion to a month's rainfall:

- An ice cream shop loses on **rainy summer weekends**: which day matters more than how much.
- A roofer loses on the **number of rain days**: one 2-inch storm costs less than ten drizzly days.
- A festival loses on **rain during specific hours**.

This is why Rainly refunds are tied to the daily and weekend contracts, and why the refund promise (not the business's loss) is what gets hedged. See [05](05-why-rainly-vs-self-hedging.md).
